# Why This Journey?

> A note on motivation. This document explains the thinking behind the roadmap in the main [README](../README.md).

---

Khayyam was a mathematician and astronomer before he was a poet. The verse I keep coming back to isn't really poetry:

*"We were not, and no flaw was in the world; we will not be, and it will be the same."*

He assumed the universe had no beginning and no end. Modern physics says otherwise: the universe started around 13.8 billion years ago, and it may well end. So both ends of his picture turned out to be wrong. But the middle part — the part about us — still holds. We're here for a short interval. Whatever we do, we do it inside that interval.

That's the setup. Not tragic, not heroic. Just the situation.

Science is the only tool I've found that keeps correcting itself when the data demands it. That self-correction is why I trust it more than any other framework for describing reality. Within science I picked robotics, and within robotics the humanoid form. Not because the human shape is special — I don't think it is — but because it's the one system whose constraints I can inspect from the inside. I know what it's like to walk, to see, to decide. So when I build one, I have something to compare against.

What I actually want to know: which parts of intelligence and having a body are general? Which principles would still hold on another planet, or with different chemistry, or on a different timescale? Which ones are universal — and which are just a product of being here?

A humanoid is a probe for those questions. What it teaches doesn't need to look like us, or stay on Earth.

I'm standing at a horizon. Everything inside it, I can measure. Everything beyond it, I can't — yet. The horizon moves when I take a step. That's the whole reason to take the step.
